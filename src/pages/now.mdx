This page is a snapshot in time. The funny thing about "now" is how quickly it becomes "then".

## Work

Work has not changed much. I am still building backend systems and caring more than I probably should about performance, reliability, and what happens after software reaches production.

Most of my tinkering lately has been around coding agents. [OpenCode](https://opencode.ai) is my go-to right now. I use [Codex](https://openai.com/codex/) too, and spent time with [Pi](https://github.com/earendil-works/pi-coding-agent), [Cline](https://cline.bot/), and a few others before settling here. OpenCode has the best mix for me: the TUI is good, I can configure almost everything, and it does not force me into somebody else's idea of a workflow.

Repeated instructions are slowly becoming skills. I have been routing tasks to different models and watching token usage instead of blindly reaching for the strongest model. Markdown files are doing more work in my setup than I expected.

I have not gone all the way into running a factory of agents. I still like seeing what is happening and controlling how the work is split. Maybe I will get there eventually, but for now I am happy tweaking the workflow myself.

## Learning

I finally started learning Rust. I wanted to understand why people consider it both special and notoriously difficult, and how it gets memory safety from compile-time checks without a garbage collector. I am going through [The Rust Programming Language](https://doc.rust-lang.org/book/) slowly, and I am beginning to understand the trade-offs. Ownership and borrowing let the compiler make strong guarantees, but they can also make seemingly ordinary code surprisingly difficult.

Rust also feels a little like a cult. It comes with its own vocabulary: ownership, borrowing, lifetimes, the borrow checker. All of it makes sense inside Rust, but very little of it transfers cleanly to the languages I already know. I am still early, so this may be an unfair diagnosis. It is interesting enough that I keep reading.

Separately, I have been cloning mature open-source repositories and stepping through them with a debugger. [SQLAlchemy](https://github.com/sqlalchemy/sqlalchemy) is the latest one. There is no particular goal beyond following real execution paths and understanding why the code is shaped the way it is.

I am reading [Refactoring](https://martinfowler.com/books/refactoring.html) by Martin Fowler as well.

## Chess

I used to [play chess professionally](https://players.chessbase.com/en/player/Devadathan_MB/435082), then stopped years ago. During Covid, I found my way back as a spectator through [ChessBase India](https://www.chessbase.in/), comedians streaming chess, and the wider boom around the game.

I kept following after that, though not very actively for the last eight or nine months. The recent [Global Chess League](https://www.globalchessleague.com/) and the ongoing [Chess Olympiad](https://chessolympiad2026.fide.com/) have pulled me back. For now, I am following games and trying to recover the enthusiasm to play.
